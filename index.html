Why Scanned Books Need a Specialized PDF Compressor

Standard online PDF compressors were designed for digital office documents with simple text and occasional photos. When applied to scanned books, they often produce bloated files (15–30 MB for a 400-page volume) or destroy text legibility with blurry JPEG compression artifacts.

Standard PDF Compressors

  • ❌ Converts text scans to 24-bit JPEGs (~30–50 KB/page)
  • ❌ Blurs serif fonts and creates noisy halo artifacts around letters
  • ❌ Often flattens and strips out searchable OCR text streams
  • ❌ Stalls or crashes on books exceeding 300+ pages

BookPDFSlim (Multi-Engine)

  • ✓ Separates Mixed Raster Content (MRC) text from background paper
  • ✓ 1-Bit Bilevel Stencil Optimizer shrinks pages to 5–10 KB
  • ✓ Keeps 100% of OCR text and page dimensions intact
  • ✓ 100% In-Browser: zero uploads, complete privacy

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I compress a book PDF to a specific target size (e.g. 10MB)?

Click the Target Size tab at the top, choose a preset (8MB, 10MB, 12MB) or type your exact limit, and drop your book. The engine automatically budgets DPI and stream compression per page to hit your target.

Will my searchable OCR text remain intact?

Yes. BookPDFSlim only compresses image and mask XObjects. All invisible OCR text layers (created by ABBYY, Tesseract, Adobe Acrobat, or Google Books) remain completely searchable and selectable.

Is it safe to upload confidential books or personal scans?

Your documents are never uploaded to any server. All processing runs locally inside your browser sandbox using WebAssembly and client-side JavaScript. Disconnecting your internet after loading the page will not affect compression.

Why do other compressors make scanned books so blurry?

Most compressors apply lossy JPEG compression across the entire page, which introduces compression ringing and fuzzy edges around black letter glyphs. BookPDFSlim converts text to binary 1-bit stencils, ensuring razor-sharp edges identical to vector type.
